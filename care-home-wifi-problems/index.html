For care home owners, managers & operations teams

Unreliable WiFi in your care home? We find the cause, then fix it.

From WiFi that drops out every afternoon to a complete network upgrade, we diagnose the problem before recommending anything — and plan the work so residents and staff are disturbed as little as possible.

Does this sound familiar?

The problems we're asked to fix most often.

  • WiFi dropping out during the day for no obvious reason
  • Slow WiFi when lots of devices are in use
  • Dead spots in certain rooms, corridors or wings
  • Staff tablets disconnecting part-way through a task
  • Care or nurse-call devices losing their connection
  • Residents unable to video call family reliably
  • Smart TVs buffering or losing their stream
  • The whole home going offline when the broadband fails
  • No one sure how the network was set up, or who to call
  • Repeated complaints from residents, families or staff

In most cases it isn't one single fault, but a network that was never designed for how much a care home now asks of it. The good news is that it's almost always fixable — usually without replacing everything.

How we approach it

Survey, diagnose, recommend — then fix.

You get a clear explanation of what's wrong and what it will take to put right before you commit to any work.

  1. Survey

    An on-site wireless and network survey — coverage, capacity, interference, roaming and configuration, measured rather than guessed at.

  2. Diagnose

    We identify the actual cause, or causes, rather than treating the most obvious symptom.

  3. Recommend

    A plain-English report: what to keep, what to reconfigure and what to replace — with the reasons for each.

  4. Fix

    If you'd like us to do the work, it's phased around the home's routine and care operations, and tested as each area is completed.

Not everything needs replacing

Spend money where it fixes the problem.

Some contractors default to ripping everything out. A proper survey tells us what can stay, which often makes the job smaller, cheaper and quicker than a full replacement.

Often reusable
Structured cabling, cabinets and containment installed to a reasonable standard
Sometimes reusable
Access points, with better placement and configuration
Usually needs attention
Network segmentation, switching and PoE capacity, and internet resilience

Staff and resident connectivity

Care systems shouldn't compete with Netflix.

When care tablets, nurse call, residents' phones, smart TVs and visitors all share one network, the busiest traffic wins. We separate them into their own networks, give care and operational systems priority, and keep guest devices well away from anything sensitive.

Why staff and resident WiFi should be separated

Phased works

Planned around the home, not the other way round.

Homes are occupied around the clock, so we agree a plan with the manager: which areas first, which tasks need quiet times, and how residents' rooms will be accessed. Each phase is finished and tested before the next starts, so the home is never left part-working.
