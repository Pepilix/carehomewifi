01 — The brief

A care home isn't an office with bedrooms.

A modern care home asks one physical network to do a dozen different jobs at once — reliably, and without any one of them interfering with another. Care and clinical systems, staff devices, residents, visitors, security, voice and entertainment, each with different needs for reliability, security and priority.

We design and build networks around that reality, rather than treating a care home like any other commercial building.

One network, carrying

  • care & staff systems.
  • residents' video calls.
  • building systems.
  • security.
  • voice.
  • entertainment.

03 — Existing care homes

Does this sound familiar?

  • WiFi dropping out during the day, for no obvious reason
  • Slow WiFi, especially when several devices are in use
  • Dead spots in certain rooms or corridors
  • Staff tablets disconnecting mid-task
  • Care or nurse-call devices losing connection
  • Residents unable to video call family reliably
  • Smart TVs buffering or losing their stream
  • Devices roaming badly between access points
  • The internet connection itself feeling unstable
  • Repeated complaints from residents, families or staff

In most cases it's not one single fault, but a network that was never designed for how much a care home now asks of it. The cause is almost always fixable — usually without replacing everything.

08 — Questions

Plain answers to common questions.

TroubleshootingWhy does care-home WiFi keep dropping out?

Usually it isn't one single fault, but a network that was never designed for how much a care home now asks of it. The common causes are access points positioned for fewer devices or a different layout, devices clinging to a weak distant access point instead of roaming, overloaded access points, resident streaming competing with operational systems, flat unsegmented networks, mixed ageing hardware, broadband that hasn't kept pace — and no monitoring, so problems are only noticed once they're disruptive.

Existing homesWill you need to replace all our hardware?

Not necessarily. We survey and diagnose the existing network first, and only recommend replacing what's genuinely causing the problem. Structured cabling, cabinets and containment can often stay; switching and segmentation are more likely to need attention. Much of the time, existing equipment can stay in place once the design around it is corrected.

Existing homesHow disruptive is a Network Health Check?

The survey itself is non-intrusive and scheduled around the home's routine. Any follow-on work is planned to avoid disrupting care operations, residents or staff shifts — often phased, as it was across the Roche Care Homes sites.

Network designWhy should staff and resident WiFi be separated?

Because they have very different needs. On a flat network, care systems, staff devices, residents and guests all share one network with no separation or prioritisation — so a resident's streaming can slow down a care tablet. A segmented network gives each category of device its own logical network, prioritised and secured on its own terms.

New buildWhen should we involve you on a new build?

As early as possible — ideally at design stage. Getting the network right during construction is significantly easier and cheaper than correcting fundamental design problems once the building is operational. We work alongside developers, main contractors, M&E consultants and architects, coordinating with other trades during first fix through to handover.

SupportDo you look after the network once it's live?

Yes. Every network is validated, tested and handed over with accurate documentation, and we offer remote monitoring, network support and ongoing management afterwards — so someone can actually see what's happening on the network before it becomes a problem.
