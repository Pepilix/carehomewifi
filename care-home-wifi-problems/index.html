The symptoms we see most often in operational care homes.

—WiFi dropping out during the day, for no obvious reason

Why it happens

Why does care-home WiFi keep dropping out?

In most cases it's not one single fault but a network that was never designed for how much a care home now asks of it. A handful of underlying causes account for the large majority of the problems we're called out to diagnose.

Insufficient coverage

Access points positioned for a smaller device count or a different building layout than what's actually in use today.

Poor roaming

Devices holding onto a weak, distant access point instead of handing over to a stronger nearby one.

Overloaded access points

Too many devices competing for one access point's capacity, even where signal strength looks fine.

Uncontrolled resident bandwidth

Streaming and personal devices consuming bandwidth that operational systems also depend on.

Flat, unsegmented networks

Care, staff, resident and guest traffic all sharing one network with no separation or prioritisation.

Ageing or inconsistent hardware

A mix of access points and switches installed at different times, often with mismatched capability.

Broadband bottlenecks

An incoming connection that was adequate when installed but hasn't kept pace with demand.

No monitoring or documentation

Nobody can see what's actually happening on the network, so problems are only noticed once they're disruptive.

Our approach

Not everything needs replacing.

A lot of network contractors default to ripping out and starting again. We diagnose first — a proper survey tells us what's actually wrong, which means we can tell you honestly what needs to stay, what needs reconfiguring, and what genuinely needs replacing.

That's often a smaller, cheaper and faster job than a full replacement — and it means any spend is going where it will actually fix the problem.

Questions

Common questions about a Health Check.

Will you need to replace all our hardware?+

Not necessarily. We survey and diagnose the existing network first, and only recommend replacing what's genuinely causing the problem. Much of the time, existing switching or cabling can stay in place once the design around it is corrected.

How disruptive is a Network Health Check?+

The survey itself is non-intrusive and is scheduled around the home's routine. Any follow-on work is planned to avoid disrupting care operations, residents or staff shifts.
