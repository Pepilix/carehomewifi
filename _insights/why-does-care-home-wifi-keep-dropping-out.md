When care home WiFi keeps dropping out, the instinct is to blame the access points and buy more of them. Sometimes that helps. More often, the dropouts have several causes working together, and new access points on their own simply move the problem around.

These are the causes we find most often when we survey an operational home.

## 1. Access points placed for a different building

Many homes were designed for a handful of office computers and a few residents' phones. Today the same network carries care tablets, nurse-call devices, monitoring sensors, staff phones, smart TVs and visitors. Access points positioned for the original layout — often in corridors, to keep cabling short — struggle to reach into bedrooms with closed fire doors.

## 2. Poor roaming between access points

Staff devices move constantly. If a tablet holds on to a distant, weak access point instead of handing over to a closer one, it will slow down and eventually disconnect. This is usually a configuration problem rather than a hardware one, and it's one of the main reasons staff report devices "dropping out mid-task".

## 3. Overloaded access points and switching

Signal strength can look fine while an access point is serving far more devices than it should. The same is true further back: a switch without enough capacity or power over Ethernet budget can cause access points to restart or slow down at busy times — often in the afternoon and evening when residents and visitors are most active.

## 4. Everything on one flat network

When care systems, staff devices, residents, guests and building systems share a single network, the busiest traffic wins. A resident streaming in one room can affect a care tablet in the next. It also means guest devices can see equipment they shouldn't. Separating these into their own network segments, with priority for care systems, solves both problems.

## 5. A single internet connection with no backup

If the broadband fails, everything that depends on the internet fails with it — cloud-based care records, monitoring alerts and video calls. We recommend fibre as the primary connection with a Starlink or 5G backup that can take over automatically where the site suits it.

## 6. Mixed, ageing hardware and no monitoring

Equipment installed at different times by different contractors rarely works well together, and without monitoring nobody can see a failing device until staff start complaining. Documentation is often missing, which makes every fault slower to find.

## What to do about it

The fix starts with a survey rather than a shopping list. Measuring coverage, capacity and roaming across the building — and looking at the switching, configuration and internet connection behind it — shows which of these causes apply. You can then keep what works, reconfigure what's poorly set up and replace only what genuinely needs it.
