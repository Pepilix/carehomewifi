Why it matters

Retrofitting a network is always harder than designing one.

Once walls are plastered and rooms are furnished, correcting poor access point positioning, insufficient cabling or a badly segmented network means disruptive, expensive remedial work in an occupied building. Designed in from the start, the same infrastructure costs less, causes no disruption, and is built around the building rather than squeezed into it.

We review architectural drawings alongside your design team, so coverage, capacity and containment are resolved on paper — before first fix, not after snagging.

The process

From design consultation to ongoing support.

The same fifteen-step process runs through every new-build project we deliver, whatever the size of the home.

01 — Design consultation

Understanding the building, the care model and the systems it will need to support.

02 — Drawing review

Working from architectural drawings to plan coverage and containment before construction starts.

03 — Wireless design

Predictive access point placement based on the building's materials, layout and expected device density.

04 — Network architecture

VLAN structure, switching topology and resilience designed around care, staff, resident and building-systems traffic.

05 — Cabling specification

Cat6 / Cat6A and fibre backbone specified to match the network architecture and future capacity.

06 — Cabinet & rack design

Communications rooms and cabinets sized and specified for the equipment they'll house.

07 — Access point positioning

Final positions agreed with the design team so APs work architecturally as well as technically.

08 — First-fix coordination

Containment and cabling routes coordinated with electrical and other trades during first fix.

09 — Installation

Access points, switching and cabling installed to specification.

10 — Configuration

VLANs, SSIDs, QoS and security policy configured before commissioning.

11 — Commissioning

The live network brought online and tested against the original design.

12 — Validation

Coverage, capacity and performance verified on site, not assumed from the design alone.

13 — Documentation

As-built records, IP schedules and configuration documentation produced for handover.

14 — Handover

A working, documented network handed over ready for the home to open.

15 — Support

Ongoing monitoring and support available once the home is operational.

Questions

Common questions from developers and contractors.

How early should we involve you in the design?+

As early as possible — ideally alongside architectural drawing development. The earlier we're involved, the more the network design can influence containment, cabinet locations and AP positions, rather than working around decisions already made.

Do you work directly with our M&E consultant or main contractor?+

Yes. We coordinate directly with the wider project team on specification, sequencing and site logistics, rather than operating as a separate, disconnected workstream.

Can the network be designed before room layouts are finalised?+

To a large extent, yes — a lot of the network architecture and capacity planning can proceed from an early-stage drawing. Final AP positions are usually confirmed once layouts are locked in.

Is this the same design for every LNT Care Developments home?+

Yes — we run a standardised WiFi and data design across every LNT home we work on, covering the complete building for residents and staff, which keeps quality and specification consistent across a development programme.

Planning a new care home?

Bring us in at design stage and we'll make sure the network is ready before the doors open.
